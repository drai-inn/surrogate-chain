How far can a chain of surrogates take us in biomolecular interactions?

Physiological outcomes come from molecules interacting; function follows structural change; pharmacology is what drugs do to that system. Machine learning now offers a surrogate at every rung, from sequence and structure to atoms and cells. Most are open-weight and most arrived recently. We climbed two rungs and measured how far each can be trusted.

01Introduction

Each rung of the machine-learning ladder is a surrogate for a calculation too expensive to run at scale, such as docking a billion molecules or sampling an ion crossing a membrane. Chained, they could take a compound to its physiological effect. The Auckland hub asked how far up you can climb before you stop trusting the answer. Two groups took rungs at opposite ends, both held to four questions: what is the ground truth, what is the simple baseline, what would change our mind, and where would it break?

02Methods

Rung 1VDSAI Lightning predicts docking fitness from the 2D graph inside an active-learning loop, so that only the top-ranked compounds are docked. It replaces AMPL's GraphConv (TensorFlow) with AttentiveFP in PyTorch Geometric and Lightning. We used scaffold splits, an Optuna study of 40 trials with a median pruner, and a test set evaluated once. The ground truth is the full docking of pool_3, a 98.3M-molecule ZINC chunk. The baselines are random picks, rank by heavy-atom count, and AMPL.

Rung 2Structure to physiology. Diffusivity D is measured from the 2nd Kramers–Moyal moment and frozen. Learning D and F together is degenerate. A port-Hamiltonian network then learns F from the drift −D∇F, so dF/dt ≤ 0 for any weights. States come from the MSM spectral gap and barriers from minimax saddles. The result is a Ce/Re bond graph with an electrical port, after Hunter et al., in which per-hop charges telescope to exactly one. It is written as CellML 2.0 and validated with libcellml. Validated on a toy and alanine dipeptide; applied to KcsA with geometry from 1K4C.

03Results

  • Faster and better. On 3.5M molecules training was 13.3× faster (2 h 26 min against 32 h 26 min). Tuned test R² was 0.752 against AMPL's 0.690, with lower RMSE and MAE. On pool_3, training was 27× faster end to end, recall of the docking top 100k was 60.4% against 56.4%, and Lightning scored the pool in 26 min on one GPU against 4 d 9 h for AMPL.
  • Billion scale. 925M ZINC molecules in 4 h 27 min, a library size this group had never screened.
  • Thermodynamics by construction. The structured cycle residual is ~10⁻¹⁵, against 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻² for an unconstrained network that fits as well. D is recovered to 0.5% on the toy, and the learned landscape correlates at r = 0.984 with a potential the network never saw. Saddle features predict rates where endpoint features cannot (ln κ ≈ ln D − βF‡).
  • Honest limits. The KcsA landscape is literature-constrained, not learned. Trapped ions never cross barriers. Conductance is ~20× low (4.5 pS against ~100 pS) because the model is single-ion and KcsA uses knock-on. We have moved to gramicidin A, now built in DMPC (area per lipid 59.3 against 60.6 Å² experimental) with all equilibration checks passing.

04Conclusions

A surrogate can beat the incumbent on accuracy and cost at once, turning billion-molecule screening into an afternoon's work. Its ceiling is now the noise in docking itself. Physical structure can be carried from atoms to an importable physiology component as an invariant rather than a penalty. Each link is only as good as its hardest region: the top tail for docking, the barrier for MD.

05Future directions

Join the rungs into one chain: screen with Lightning, pose the hits with co-folding (Boltz-2, OpenFold3), simulate them, build the bond graph, and run a cell model. Raise the screening ceiling with consensus docking and 3D or MLIP rescoring. Learn barriers with an SRV coordinate and weighted-ensemble sampling on gramicidin A. Model drug block and mutations as bond-graph perturbations in cardiac and renal cell models.
